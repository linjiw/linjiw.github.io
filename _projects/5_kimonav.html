Let the body choose how.

The public task carries a destination and optional route, heading, arrival and hold requirements. Perception describes what is known. Planning chooses a continuation. The tracker executes it, and measured feedback updates the remaining task.

Allowed at deployment

Public goal/map, measured initial body, current orientation and actual motor history; fixed previously built motion library.

Training or diagnostic information

Exact map/localization assumptions are declared. Future realized robot motion and a task-provided answer reference do not select the candidate.

Robot input

“Reach this region and wait.” Optional heading, time window, route and allowed contacts. Sensors provide joint state, IMU and later RGB/depth/LiDAR, each with its frame, timestamp and availability.

Robot output

Physical whole-body motion plus task status: progress, completion, a replanning request or no supported continuation. Joint commands are the motor output; motion references are the planner output.

Navigation tokens, predicted motion and SONIC code64 have different meanings. The current R640 motor interface does not automatically accept world goals, scene geometry or new contacts. Proposed versioned input/output contract →

03 / Why the body belongs in navigation

The same route.
A different body decision.

A narrow opening can require rotation. An overhang can require lowering the body. When both occur, the movements must be coordinated. The important experiment changes the geometry while keeping the destination fixed.

Width is blocked. Rotate the illustrative body to change the required clearance.

Geometric illustration only. Dimensions describe a simplified envelope, not measured G1 geometry. Passing these width/height tests does not establish balance, foot placement, dynamic feasibility or collision-free robot execution.

Initial body dexterity

Coordinate torso, arms, pelvis, legs and feet to change swept volume, using supported motions and permitted floor contact.

Separate contact extension

Hand-supported climbing, opening doors, pushing objects and carrying loads require contact-aware dynamics and motor qualification.

Future observation extension

Keep support, obstacles, overhead space and unknown regions explicit. Sensor adapters must retain these meanings and be tested under noise and delay.

04 / Experiments that change a decision

A stable goal.
A replaceable method.

Each stage has an observable exit condition. A planner–tracker is the main proposed route; direct student compression remains a comparison. Registered execution studies now qualify parts of this route; the latest fixed-duration comparison retains the failure. See the complete progress record above.

Establish task feasibility

Compare original and repaired motors. Separate supplied-reference tracking from standing-start navigation. Keep historical timing scores; define new destination and hold tasks before testing.

DECISIONCan a motor execute the required continuation reliably? If not, improve motion/support capability before teaching a navigator.

Resolve sparse control

One bounded comparison: task-driven short-reference planner, measured-reference student and direct-code baseline. Use fresh fit-only states with matched replay and teacher-data controls.

DECISIONPromote full task-and-quality gains. If only planning works, proceed with it. Lower MSE alone does not justify another training cycle.

Test geometry-dependent choices

Reuse verified scene–motion assets. Keep start and destination fixed while changing narrow, overhead and ground constraints. Compare planar control with body-aware planning.

DECISIONDoes body geometry improve contact-free completion without sacrificing open-space behavior? If a simpler method ties, use it.

Replace ideal observations

Introduce partial depth/LiDAR and later RGB-based estimates. Test occlusion, drift, latency and dropout with task and motor held fixed. Let physics continue during computation.

DECISIONMeasure completion, false feasibility admissions and useful coverage. Plug-compatible input shapes are not evidence of robust perception.

Extend composition and control

Test longer routes, changed future commands, optional timing and independently authored language. Add new contacts and embodiments only with corresponding motor support.

DECISIONHold out the task, scene or condition named in the claim. Keep hardware and cross-robot results separate from simulator repeats.

05 / The comparison set has changed

The goal is useful.
The broad idea is shared.

Whole-body traversal, scene-conditioned references and planner–tracker hierarchies already have close precedents. Our proposed contribution needs a specific advantage under matched task and observation conditions.

TANGO · 8 SEPTEMBER 2026

Whole-body visual navigation

The closest RGB-and-language reference-chunk comparison. Study its task and motor interfaces before claiming a new general navigation system.

PASSAGE · 16 SEPTEMBER 2026

Perceptive planner–tracker traversal

A direct comparator for destination-conditioned planning and body geometry. Our modular architecture alone is not the contribution.

CAT / HUMANOIDPF · 2026

Body–obstacle relationships

Compare explicit per-body geometry with simpler planar control at matched sensing and execution capability.

MTC · OUR RELATED DATA WORK

Scene-aligned motion as supervision

Connect to existing geometry–motion data. Keep captured motion, retargeted reference and physical execution distinct.
